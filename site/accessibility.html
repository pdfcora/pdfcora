Accessibility

Documents people can
actually read.

Two halves: the viewer your users touch, and the PDFs your app produces. Both are tested — the viewer by automated audits in the gates, the documents by veraPDF against PDF/UA-1.

The viewer

A page bitmap tells a screen reader nothing. So we expose the structure instead.

The rendered page is hidden from assistive technology, and the document’s own structure is published beside it: headings as headings, lists as lists, figures with their alternate text, table cells, form fields — in reading order.

iOS

VoiceOver

Elements with the right traits and sort priority, so the reading order follows the document rather than the layout.

Android

TalkBack

Compose semantics with a traversal index, checked node by node in the gates against the tree the platform hands a screen reader.

Web

Screen readers and keyboard

Real buttons, a visible focus ring, and +, −, Page Up and Page Down while the page has focus. Sized in rem, so the toolbar follows the reader’s text size.

In the gates

Audited on every run

axe-core over the mounted web component in three states, XCUIApplication.performAccessibilityAudit() on three iOS screens, and the Accessibility Test Framework over Android’s own accessibility tree. These found real defects — unnamed figures, unlabelled marks, low contrast, clipped text — which is why they run every time.

The documents

Untagged in, PDF/UA out.

  1. Read what is there

    The structure tree, if the document has one, and whether it is tagged at all.

  2. Propose tags

    Rules over the page objects group text into headings, paragraphs, list items, figures and artifacts, in reading order, with a proposed level for each heading.

  3. Describe the figures

    Figures with no alternate text are listed with the page text around them, described on device where the platform can, and written as /Alt — or marked decorative.

  4. Write and verify

    Accepted proposals are written as a real structure tree with a parent tree, title, language and an XMP packet claiming PDF/UA-1 — then veraPDF says whether that claim is true.

Measured on real files

238 documents nobody wrote for us.

Contracts from SEC filings, arXiv papers, EU Official Journal acts in English and German, German statistical reports, an invoice guide and a slice of the GovDocs1 corpus — 6,146 pages, judged by veraPDF.

Nothing crashed

All 238 opened. 13 were refused with a typed error — 9 encrypted, 4 with a broken cross-reference table. 762,178 blocks were proposed across the corpus in 31 seconds.

A quarter were already tagged

61 documents, correctly refused rather than re-tagged: every EU act, most arXiv papers, five of six German statistical reports. Contracts never are.

Nine came out compliant

Of 160 remediated, 9 passed PDF/UA-1 outright. 129 of the rest fail only on figures needing descriptions, untagged annotations, or fonts the source never embedded.

Fonts are the ceiling

105 documents use fonts that were never embedded. No amount of tagging embeds a font, so those files cannot reach PDF/UA through remediation at all — worth knowing before promising a customer otherwise.

The survey paid for itself twice

It found two defects a hand-written fixture never could: the trial watermark was untagged content on every page, so a screen reader would read it in the middle of the text; and heading levels came from font size alone, so a document whose largest line was not its first heading started below H1 and skipped levels. Both are fixed on all three platforms, with a shared fixture to keep them fixed.

What is missing

The honest list.

  • No independent audit yet. The conformance report is a vendor self-assessment from automated and scripted testing. It is not a VPAT® and should not be published as one.
  • No screen reader has been driven by a person. Not VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA or JAWS. That is the gap an audit must close.
  • Tables become paragraphs and vector charts become artifacts, so a chart is not announced.
  • Annotations and form fields are not tagged, so a page with links will not pass PDF/UA after remediation.
  • Reading order is content order. A multi-column page drawn out of order is not reordered — the reviewer sees the numbers and can fix them.
  • No OCR, so a scanned page has no text to tag.
